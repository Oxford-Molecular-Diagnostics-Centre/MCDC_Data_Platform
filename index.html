Overview

One research lifecycle, connected data

MCDC projects may start with a detailed clinical cohort, a study-specific data collection portal or only a pseudonymised subject identifier. Laboratory and analytical systems are then selected and configured to match the approved study design.

01 / PLAN
Clinical cohortClinical records, subject IDs, study metadata
02 / COLLECT
Biological samplesRecruitment, receipt, processing and storage
03 / TRACK
Laboratory LIMSBarcodes, aliquots, metadata and audit records
04 / GENERATE
Molecular dataSequencing, immune assays and histology
05 / ANALYSE
BioinformaticsQC, pipelines and data interpretation
06 / DELIVER
Research outputsReports, analysis files, controlled transfer
Flexible by design. The exact data journey, clinical metadata requirements, system access and deliverables are defined with each project team. Not every study uses every platform, and some connections involve controlled import/export rather than a live integration.

01 · Study data

Clinical data and cohort management

Study information can be captured in REDCap, a dedicated clinical data portal or project-specific forms. Other projects may require only a subject ID and minimal metadata for laboratory linkage.

Study-specific clinical data capture

Data inputs are agreed with collaborators and can include cohort records, collection dates, study visits, sample identifiers and appropriate clinical variables.

  • REDCap or bespoke study portals when required.
  • Pseudonymised subject identifiers and agreed metadata fields.
  • Controlled imports, exports and study-specific access permissions.
  • Data handling consistent with study approvals and governance requirements.

Example: TriOx uses a dedicated study data collection form linked to its sample and results workflow.

Status: Sample registration and laboratory traceability

Traceability through processing

Our LIMS provides a framework for tracking received material and derivatives through laboratory workflows, not just at the point of intake.

  • Sample identifiers, tubes, aliquots and parent–child relationships.
  • Storage, box-scanning, sample metadata and audit records.
  • Quarantine and project-specific access controls depend on the selected workflow.
  • Expansion across NGS, tissue bank and immunology workflows.

Additional workflows are implemented incrementally with user testing, documentation and validation appropriate to intended use.

03 · Storage & computing

Hybrid data infrastructure

Research data can remain on local or institutional systems where appropriate and use dedicated cloud resources for scalable computational analysis, archiving and approved external collaboration.

01

Local & institutional systems

Instrument-connected workstations, laboratory and office computing, on-premises processing, controlled shared drives and SharePoint collaboration.

02

Amazon Web Services

Project-specific S3 storage, cloud compute, data archiving and access controls. Cost attribution and usage monitoring are supported through project tagging.

03

Illumina cloud ecosystem

BaseSpace Sequence Hub (BSSH), Illumina Connected Analytics (ICA) and Connected Insights (ICI) capabilities for sequencing-related workflows.

Collaborator access and data delivery

Secure project-specific access can be arranged through controlled cloud resources, permissions and agreed data-transfer routes. File formats, metadata, storage location and retention arrangements are specified with each project.

Local + AWS + Illumina
Data governance: handling, retention and transfer are defined through applicable approvals and agreements. Access and storage policies, backup and recovery planning, and standardised change control continue to be formalised across MCDC systems.

05 · Quality & governance

Traceability and fit-for-purpose control

Digital systems are developed and managed with appropriate attention to auditability, reproducibility, access control and data integrity. Laboratory and study-specific requirements determine the level of documentation, testing and validation required.

Supporting practices include controlled SOPs, audit trails, role-based access, system change records, computerised system validation and data retention planning.

Important scope distinction: an individual laboratory's accreditation does not imply accreditation of the entire MCDC Data Platform. System validation and regulatory expectations are assessed per study, assay and intended use.
ISO 15189Relevant to genomics laboratory quality and applicable workflows.
GCLPQuality expectations for applicable clinical laboratory research.
HTA / GCPApplicable tissue governance, research and clinical study requirements.
ALCOA+Data integrity principles guiding traceable computerised records.
06 · Oxford-wide collaboration

Connecting laboratory data with translational research

MCDC's laboratory-facing infrastructure complements the Oxford Cancer Translational Data Platform (TDP), which brings together curated clinical, genomic, imaging and molecular datasets for translational cancer research.

For suitable collaborations, the teams can explore how MCDC-generated data may be made compatible with wider cohort analysis, visualisation and reuse, subject to project agreements and governance approvals.
